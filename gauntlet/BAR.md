# The Bar: No More Room in Hell — Survival mode

This is the reference the gauntlet loop judges against. The bar exists in two
forms: **five real NMRiH screenshots supplied by the project owner** (pinned
locally for the critics; not committed — they are the game's copyrighted
material), and the sourced written specification below. Critics view the
actual screenshots side by side with ours and are instructed that a tie or a
doubt is a loss for our game.

## Mechanics checklist (measurable half of the bar)

- **M1 — Wave survival.** Survivors hold out against escalating waves of the
  horde while defending safe zones.
- **M2 — Zombie variety.** Waves mix *walkers* (slow, relentless), *runners*
  (fast, terrifying), and *kids/crawlers*. One shape of zombie is below the bar.
- **M3 — Supply drops.** After a set number of waves a helicopter performs a
  supply drop where players stock up on weapons, ammunition and supplies —
  resupply is an event, not random litter.
- **M4 — Respawn tokens.** A respawn token is earned every 20 zombies killed
  (10 on casual); death is otherwise sticky.
- **M5 — Barricading.** With a hammer and boards, players board up entry
  points; barricades are breakable and buy time.
- **M6 — Melee and shove.** Melee is a real fallback with commitment and
  stamina cost; a shove creates space without killing.
- **M7 — Ammo friction.** Ammo is scarce and has weight; the ammunition count
  is only shown when reloading or deliberately checking — counting bullets is
  part of the fear.
- **M8 — Infection.** A bite can infect; the infected player's vision darkens
  with creeping veins and they choose whether to tell the team.
- **M9 — Wounds and healing.** First aid kits and bandages heal; bleeding is a
  state, not just a number going down.
- **M10 — Minimal HUD.** No crosshair. Almost nothing on screen; health is
  read from how dark and desaturated the screen is, not from a bar.
- **M11 — Darkness as a mechanic.** Maps are genuinely dark; the flashlight is
  a survival tool, and visibility is a resource.
- **M12 — Co-op.** Up to eight survivors live or die as a group.

## Visual and atmosphere specification (taste half of the bar)

- **V1 — Muted palette.** Desaturated, grim colors; "muted color palettes and
  strategic lighting choices reinforce isolation and impending doom."
- **V2 — Oppressive darkness.** "Players are running around in the dark with
  only a flashlight to guide them"; every dark corner and flickering light
  keeps you on edge. Light is scarce, directional, and meaningful.
- **V3 — Grim, decayed world.** Familiar places gone wrong — urban decay,
  abandonment, fog; an "immersive, bleak world where darkness creeps in around
  every corner" (Romero-style dread, not action-movie spectacle).
- **V4 — Grounded gore.** Blood and death feedback is heavy and unglamorous;
  kills feel costly, corpses persist.
- **V5 — Post-processing dread.** Film-grain, vignette, contrast: the screen
  itself communicates fear and failing health.
- **V6 — Readable silhouettes.** Even in darkness, threats read instantly by
  shape and motion, never by UI markers.

## How the critic judges

One piece at a time, fresh context, binary verdict: does our running game's
actual output beat what this specification describes for that piece —
translated honestly to a top-down 2D browser game? "Would pass as a scene
from a NMRiH-quality survival experience in its own medium" is the standard.
A tie is a loss. Praise is not useful; the verdict names the single biggest
remaining gap.
